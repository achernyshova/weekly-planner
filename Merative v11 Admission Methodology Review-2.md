# Merative v11 Admission Methodology Review

**New / target:** Merative v11 — *Truven Flexible Analytics Inpatient Admission Grouper Methodology Guide v11.2.0*  
**Legacy / baseline:** Merative Advantage Suite v5.4 — *Data Manager’s Guide*, Chapter 9

## Purpose and summary

This review summarizes the highest-impact documented differences and alignments between the legacy Advantage Suite v5.4 admission methodology and Merative v11. Both methodologies organize facility and professional medical activity into single-patient inpatient admissions centered on facility room-and-board activity. They do **not**, however, document identical rules for identifiers, claim eligibility, window construction, LOS, admission-level field selection, clinical processing, deconstruction, or outputs.

The cited rules establish **methodology differences**, not observed production results. Where the documents do not provide a complete cross-version comparison, this review says **unknown/not documented**. A different population, split/merge pattern, claim assignment, identifier, or calculated value is described only as **possible** when it depends on source data, configuration, or undocumented behavior.

Page citations use `NEW p. n` for the v11 guide and `LEGACY p. 9-n` for Chapter 9 of the legacy guide.

## Admission ID

The documents specify different Admission ID constructions.

**Legacy v5.4 — verified formula**

```text
Admit ID =
  1. Patient Key
  + 2. Admit Start Period Key
  + 3. Two-digit sequential number
```

Legacy expressly calls this a **concatenation** and a **unique identifier**. The two-digit sequence distinguishes multiple admissions sharing Patient and Date Keys. The ID remains the same when admissions are updated or rebuilt unless admission dates change. **Reference: LEGACY p. 9-9.**

**Merative v11 — verified formula**

```text
admission_id =
  1. Patient sequence number, starting from one
  followed by
  2. Seven-digit number calculated from the admission date
```

V11 documents these two components and no third component. **“Followed by”** is the source language; the page does not expressly call the operation concatenation. **Reference: NEW p. 36.**

The construction specifications therefore **differ**. An observed cross-version ID mismatch is possible even when admissions are otherwise comparable, but the two pages do not prove that every matched admission will have a different ID or provide a cross-version matching rule. V11 p. 36 also does not state a counterpart to legacy’s explicit same-patient/same-date disambiguation condition, exact field length, data type, uniqueness property, or rerun behavior; those points are **unknown**, not proven differences.

V11 does document an interface constraint: when admissions and episodes are built in the same run, generated admissions and claims not built into admissions are sent to the episode grouper in one buffer, so the admission ID field must accommodate generated IDs and the input-medical-claim `Claim ID` field must be the same length. The legacy page states no comparable field-length condition. The constraint is confirmed; its effect on actual ID values is unknown. **References: NEW p. 36; LEGACY p. 9-9.**

## What remains the same or generally aligned

“Generally aligned” means the documents share a rule’s purpose or structure; it does not mean identical predicates, fields, source precedence, or outputs.

| Area | What remains aligned | Qualification | References |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Overall purpose and window foundation** | Both summarize single-patient inpatient confinement and use facility room-and-board activity to establish admission windows before evaluating other claims. | V11 additionally states `claim_type_cd`, inpatient-place, and valid-`rb_indicator` conditions; detailed eligibility is not equivalent. | LEGACY pp. 9-1, 9-5; NEW pp. 17, 28, 32 |
| **One-day adjacency** | Both define adjacency as a record begin date exactly one day after current discharge and run transfer logic. | The pages do not prove identical downstream processing in every non-transfer case. | LEGACY p. 9-7; NEW pp. 29–30 |

## Bottom line

The methodologies retain the same overall admission-grouping purpose and several common structural concepts, but they do not document full parity. The clearest confirmed differences are the Admission ID formulas, stated eligibility predicates, v11 room-and-board derivation, legacy Begin Date precedence, the discharge-boundary branch, multi-admission routing, LOS calculation, first-versus-latest field sourcing, diagnosis/ICD processing, admission-gap edge values, and output interfaces.

These rule differences make claim reassignment, admission split/merge, ID mismatch, population change, or calculated-value change **possible** under the relevant data conditions; the documents alone do not prove that any one of those outcomes occurs universally or in a specific production population. Where one methodology is silent—particularly for v11 reconstruction, all-missing defaults, acute-only scope, deconstruction thresholds, or per-diem trim—the correct conclusion is **not documented/unknown**, not that the behavior is absent.
